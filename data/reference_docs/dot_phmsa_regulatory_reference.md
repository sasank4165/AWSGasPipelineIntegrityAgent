# DOT PHMSA Pipeline Safety Regulatory Reference
## 49 CFR Part 191 — Transportation of Natural Gas by Pipeline
## Incident Reporting Requirements & PHMSA Form 7100.1

---

## 1. Regulatory Authority & Applicability

### 1.1 Governing Regulations
- **49 CFR Part 191**: Transportation of Natural Gas by Pipeline — Annual Reports, Incident Reports, and Safety-Related Condition Reports
- **49 CFR Part 192**: Transportation of Natural and Other Gas by Pipeline — Minimum Federal Safety Standards
- **Administering agency**: U.S. Department of Transportation, Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA)
- **Applicability**: All operators of natural gas transmission pipelines in interstate and intrastate commerce

### 1.2 Key Definitions (49 CFR 191.3)
- **Incident**: An event involving a gas pipeline facility that results in: (1) a death or personal injury requiring hospitalization; (2) estimated property damage of $50,000 or more; (3) unintentional estimated gas loss of 3 million cubic feet or more; (4) an event that is significant in the judgment of the operator
- **Significant incident**: Any event the operator determines warrants reporting based on potential consequences
- **Transmission line**: A pipeline, other than a gathering line, that transports gas in interstate or intrastate commerce

---

## 2. Incident Reporting Requirements (49 CFR 191.5)

### 2.1 Immediate Notification — 5-Minute Rule
An operator must give notice of each incident **as soon as practicable but no later than 1 hour** after confirmed detection to:

**DOT PHMSA National Response Center (NRC)**
- Phone: **1-800-424-8802** (24 hours/day, 7 days/week)
- Available: All hours, all days including holidays

**Incidents requiring immediate notification:**
- Death or injury requiring hospitalization
- Estimated property damage ≥ $50,000 (including cost of gas lost)
- Uncontrolled release of gas (any volume) that results in fire or explosion
- Unintentional estimated gas loss ≥ 3 MMSCF
- Emergency shutdown of pipeline facilities
- Any event the operator determines is significant

### 2.2 Written Report — 30-Day Requirement
A written report on **PHMSA Form 7100.1** must be submitted within **30 days** of the incident to:
- Online: PHMSA Portal at https://portal.phmsa.dot.gov
- Mail: PHMSA, Information Resources Manager, PHP-10, 1200 New Jersey Ave SE, Washington, DC 20590

### 2.3 Supplemental Report
If new information becomes available after the initial report, a supplemental report must be filed within 30 days of obtaining the new information.

## 4. Leak Rate Thresholds & Reporting Triggers

### 4.1 Reporting Decision Matrix

| Leak Rate | Estimated Volume | Reporting Required | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any rate with injury/death | Any | Yes — NRC + Form 7100.1 | NRC within 1 hour; written within 30 days |
| Any rate with fire/explosion | Any | Yes — NRC + Form 7100.1 | NRC within 1 hour; written within 30 days |
| >3 MMSCF total release | >3,000 MCF | Yes — NRC + Form 7100.1 | NRC within 1 hour; written within 30 days |
| Property damage ≥$50,000 | Any | Yes — Form 7100.1 | Written within 30 days |
| Operator-determined significant | Any | Yes — NRC + Form 7100.1 | NRC within 1 hour; written within 30 days |
| Minor release, no consequences | <3 MMSCF, no damage | No federal report required | Document internally |

### 4.2 Volume Conversion Reference
- 1 MMSCFD = 1,000 MCF/day = 1,000,000 SCF/day
- 3 MMSCF threshold = 3,000 MCF = 3,000,000 SCF
- At 0.5 MMSCFD leak rate: threshold reached in 6 days
- At 2.0 MMSCFD leak rate: threshold reached in 1.5 days
- At 5.0 MMSCFD leak rate: threshold reached in 14.4 hours

## 5. Safety-Related Condition Reports (49 CFR 191.23)

### 5.1 When Required
An operator must submit a Safety-Related Condition Report within **5 days** of confirming any of the following:
- General deterioration of pipeline that could lead to imminent hazard
- Discovered defect that could cause failure if not corrected
- Operating pressure exceeding MAOP
- Unintended movement or abnormal loading of pipeline

### 5.2 Submission
- Form: PHMSA Form 7100.2
- Submit to: PHMSA Office of Pipeline Safety, PHP-10
- Also notify: State pipeline safety authority (Texas Railroad Commission for TX pipelines)

## 6. Texas Railroad Commission (TxRRC) Requirements

### 6.1 State Reporting (in addition to federal)
Texas intrastate pipelines must also report to:
- **Texas Railroad Commission, Pipeline Safety Division**
- Phone: (512) 463-6838
- Online: https://www.rrc.texas.gov/pipeline-safety/

### 6.2 TxRRC Reporting Thresholds
- Same triggers as federal (49 CFR 191.5) plus:
- Any release that results in public evacuation
- Any release within 1,000 feet of a school, hospital, or public building
- Any release that results in media coverage

### 6.3 TxRRC Form P-18 (Incident Report)
Required within 30 days for all reportable incidents. Fields mirror PHMSA Form 7100.1 with additional Texas-specific fields for:
- RRC pipeline permit number
- RRC district office notification
- Texas county and survey information

## 8. Post-Incident Requirements

### 8.1 Incident Investigation
Per 49 CFR 192.617, operators must:
- Investigate each incident to determine cause
- Review procedures to determine if changes are needed
- Implement corrective actions within 1 year
- Document investigation findings and corrective actions

### 8.2 Return to Service
Before returning an isolated segment to service after a leak:
1. Repair or replace damaged pipe section
2. Pressure test repaired section to 1.25× MAOP for minimum 8 hours
3. Inspect repair with appropriate NDE (radiography or ultrasonic)
4. Document repair in pipeline records
5. Notify PHMSA of return to service if incident was reportable
6. Update ILI baseline if repair affects anomaly assessment

### 8.3 Record Retention
- Incident reports: retain for life of pipeline
- SCADA data for incident period: retain minimum 5 years
- Repair records: retain for life of pipeline
- Pressure test records: retain for life of pipeline
